# DRAFT — Editorial independence and conflicts of interest

> **Status: DRAFT for discussion. Not published, not in force, not linked from the
> site.** Prepared 2026-09-30 by the atlas maintainer for review and sign-off by
> MassTech (Massachusetts AI Hub), Red Hat and IBM (IBM Ventures), and The Open
> Accelerator. Nothing here binds anyone until the sign-off table at the end is
> complete and the final text is published.

## Why this exists

Founders use the Boston AI Atlas to decide where to go for money, space, compute and
advice. Its publisher is funded by some of the organizations it lists. The atlas is only
useful if founders can trust that those organizations get the same treatment as
everyone else. This policy says how.

## 1. Who publishes and who sponsors

- **Publisher:** The Open Accelerator (TOA) publishes the atlas and its maintainers
  decide what it says.
- **Sponsors:** TOA is an initiative of the Massachusetts AI Hub (a program of MassTech),
  Red Hat and IBM Ventures. In this policy they are "the sponsors".
- The data is open (CC BY 4.0), the code is open (MIT), and every change is made in
  public in the atlas's GitHub repository, with its history.

## 2. Same rules for everyone

- An organization is listed if it is real, active, in Greater Boston or Massachusetts,
  and relevant to AI founders — and a maintainer has checked that. The sponsors are
  listed on the same terms, not because they are sponsors.
- Every entry follows the same format, length limits, fact-checking and review. Entry
  text is factual; the "why it matters" line says what a founder can get and what the
  catch is, without selling. That applies to sponsors' entries at least as strictly.
- The same rules decide when an entry is corrected or removed.

## 3. No paid placement

Nobody can pay, sponsor or partner their way into the atlas, into a better position in
it, or into different wording. Sponsoring TOA buys nothing in the atlas. The atlas
carries no paid advertising. Two things on the page are not neutral listings, and are
marked as such: the publisher's own promotion (the call to apply to the TOA residency)
and the sponsors' logos in the footer.

## 4. No sponsor review before publication

Sponsor organizations do not see, review or approve content before it is published —
including entries about themselves, their competitors, or the founder tips. After
publication they can ask for changes the same way anyone can (section 7), in public.

A maintainer who is employed by a sponsor edits and approves as a maintainer only — never
on their employer's behalf, and never passing drafts to their employer for comment — and
works under the limits in section 7.

## 5. Sponsor entries are labelled

Entries connected to the publisher or a sponsor carry a visible label wherever the entry
is shown in detail, and the same field appears in the public data downloads:

- **Atlas publisher** — TOA.
- **Atlas sponsor** — a sponsor, or the legal entity it belongs to.
- **Sponsor-affiliated** — a lab, program or division a sponsor funds, co-runs or
  contains, which is not itself a sponsor.

The label is a disclosure, not a highlight: it never makes an entry bigger, brighter,
earlier in a list or more connected than the facts support.

## 6. Ranking, matching and any future assistant are sponsor-blind

- Search results, sorting, filters, map and graph views and the "I need…" matching never
  use sponsorship as an input.
- Founder tips point to the kinds of options in the atlas, not to one sponsor's program.
- If the atlas ever adds personalised matching or an AI assistant, the same rule applies:
  sponsorship is not an input, the instructions it runs on are published, and before
  launch it is tested for sponsor bias (for example, asking the same question about
  comparable sponsor and non-sponsor options and comparing the answers). The results are
  published.

## 7. Sponsors take part like anyone else

- Sponsors suggest new entries and flag mistakes through the same public forms as
  everyone else, and those requests are judged by the same rules.
- A maintainer who works for a sponsor says so publicly (in a maintainer list in the
  repository), and does not approve changes to their own employer's entry, other
  sponsors' entries or the founder tips on their own: a second maintainer must approve
  (once there is one — see open questions).

## 8. Corrections and takedowns

- **Anyone can flag an entry** from the atlas (🚩 on every entry) or on GitHub. A flag
  with a source is checked first. We aim to act on factual corrections within 14 days
  and say what we did, and why, on the flag itself.
- **An organization can ask to be removed.** We remove it once we have confirmed the
  request really comes from that organization.
- **Legal or safety problems** (for example, personal information that should not be
  public) are taken down first and reviewed after.
- Disagreements are decided by the maintainers, in public, with the reason written down.
  Sponsors have no veto.

## 9. Review

This policy is reviewed once a year, and whenever a sponsor joins or leaves. Changes are
proposed in public, dated, and recorded in the repository's history.

---

## Open questions for sign-off

1. **Second maintainer.** Section 7's two-person rule needs a second maintainer, ideally
   not employed by a sponsor. Who, and by when? Until then, how are changes to sponsor
   entries checked — and, if the only maintainer works for a sponsor, how does section 4
   hold in practice (for example: the disclosure in section 7 is made now, sponsor-entry
   changes stay open as public pull requests for a set period before merging, and an
   outside reviewer is named)?
2. **Where it lives.** Published on the atlas site, in the repository, or both — and
   linked from the footer?
3. **Correction time.** Is 14 days the right target for a volunteer-maintained project?
4. **Removal requests.** Should public bodies and state programs be removable on request,
   or only private organizations?
5. **Funding disclosure.** Should the policy state the sponsorship arrangement (in-kind,
   financial, or both) in plain terms?
